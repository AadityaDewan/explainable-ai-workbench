Which explanations can practitioners trust?

  • Treat agreement as evidence, not proof. If SHAP, LIME and permutation importance agree (high rank correlation and top-5 overlap), the finding is more credible. Agreement among methods that share a failure mode, such as correlated features, is weaker evidence.
  • Check stability first. An explanation that changes under 5% noise when the prediction does not change is a sampling artefact. Compare the perturbed score to the seed-only baseline.
  • Counterfactuals are the most actionable and are faithful by construction (the prediction really flips), but they show one of many possible changes and may suggest unrealistic values.
  • Permutation importance and PDP are global. They say nothing reliable about a single case, and are distorted by correlated features (this dataset has many).
  • Grad-CAM shows where the network looked, not why. Check it against noise and wrong predictions before relying on it.
  • Use the Disagreement tab to find cases where methods diverge, and the user study to check whether practitioners actually understand and over- or under-trust each format.
